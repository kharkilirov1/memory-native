# mx6 GPU numbers — 2026-10-30 (A100-SXM4-40GB)

First measurements after the 2026-10-30 correctness work (accumulator packed into the
code word, real bit packing, working `enable_compile`). Everything below is measured,
not projected. Harness: `probes/bench_gpu.py`.

**Setup.** A100-SXM4-40GB (sm_80), torch 2.11.0+cu130, Triton 3.6.0. Stack of two
linears `1024 -> 4096 -> 1024`, batch 64 (65k tokens/step), 40 timed steps after 10
warmup, fp32 unless stated. One step = forward + backward + state update (the counter
arms update inside backward; the dense arms also call `optimizer.step()`).

## Speed, peak memory, training state

| arm | ms/step | tok/s | vs bf16 | peak MB | bits/param |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| dense fp32 + AdamW | 1.04 | 62.9M | 1.25x | 177.8 | 96.00 |
| **dense bf16 (fp32 master) + AdamW** | **1.30** | 50.2M | **1.00x** | 177.7 | 96.00 |
| dense bf16 + `torch.compile` | 1.53 | 42.9M | 0.85x | 177.7 | 96.00 |
| PackedNA5 (eager) | 8.73 | 7.5M | 0.15x | 389.3 | 5.26 |
| PackedNA6 (eager) | 5.58 | 11.7M | 0.23x | 388.0 | 6.26 |
| **PackedNA6 + `enable_compile()`** | **3.92** | 16.7M | **0.33x** | **168.0** | 6.26 |
| PackedNA6 fused decode-in-GEMM | 21.44 | 3.1M | 0.06x | 356.0 | 6.26 |
| MXFP4 9-state (dense codes) | 6.44 | 10.2M | 0.20x | 404.4 | 8.26 |

Reading:

- **Persistent state is the real win and it is now honest**: 6.26 bits/param measured
  against 96 bits for bf16 + AdamW (fp32 master 4 B + two moments 8 B) — **15x smaller**.
  (An earlier draft of this table compared 6.27 *bits* against 12 *bytes* and claimed
  1.9x; that was a unit error, corrected here.)
- **Training peak is worse, not better**, until the dense decode is removed: 388 MB vs
  178 MB (2.2x). `torch.compile` fixes this — it fuses the decode, so peak falls to
  168 MB, i.e. 1.3x *below* dense.
- **Speed is 3.0x off bf16 after `torch.compile`**, not the 1.6x the older document
  reported. The 0.62x/0.73x figures predate this work (dense `uint8` codes, separate
  accumulator, inert compile flag) and must not be quoted.

## `torch.compile` is the lever, and it needs a batch

| batch | dense bf16 | NA6 eager | NA6 fused | NA6 + compile |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.24 | 5.43 | 7.82 | 6.57 |
| 8 | 1.26 | 5.50 | 10.19 | 8.46 |
| 32 | 1.29 | 5.44 | 8.44 | **3.94** |
| 128 | 1.27 | 5.82 | 24.94 | **4.76** |

Compile wins from batch >= 32 (1.38x over eager) and loses below it. The operating
envelope should say so.

## Negative result: fused decode-in-GEMM does not pay

The fused kernel (`mx6/triton_kernel.py`) is **correct** — 15/15 parity tests against the
fp32 reference at atol/rtol 1e-4, for both the forward (`packed_matmul`) and grad_x
(`packed_matmul_dx`) — but it is **slower at every batch size tested**: 7.82 vs 5.43 ms
at M=1, 24.94 vs 5.82 ms at M=128.

Cause: the dense path decodes the weight once per step (0.357 ms) and hands it to
cuBLAS. Decoding inside the GEMM repeats that work for every output tile, and the
weight is only ~16 MB — small enough that materialising it is cheaper than re-decoding.
The kernel is therefore kept for small-VRAM inference, not as a training default
(`fused="off"`).

## Where the remaining time goes (one 1024->4096 layer, batch 32)

| phase | ms | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| update math (ticks, SR, carry) | 1.619 | 53% |
| pack + unpack | 0.274 | 9% |
| decode | 0.357 | 12% |
| forward matmul + backward + rest | 0.780 | 26% |
| **full step** | **3.030** | |

The updater is the only remaining lever of size. A fused update kernel (grad_w
correlation, RMS, ticks, stochastic rounding, carry, pack in one launch) is the next
piece of work; the decode and packing pieces are already small.

## Not measured

- 8-bit Adam / bitsandbytes was not available in the runtime image.
- Quality was not re-run here; the char-LM A/B is unchanged by these kernels
  (`tests/test_packed_na.py::test_charlm` still passes) but a full 3000-step sweep was
  not repeated.
- Multi-layer whole-model numbers (a real transformer) were not taken; this is a
  two-layer stack.
